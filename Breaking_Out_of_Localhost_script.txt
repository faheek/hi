You've just built the perfect app.
The frontend is flawless, and the database is saving every single click.
But the second you send that link to a friend,
the site instantly crashes on their phone.
That's because addresses like port 5173 aren't real locations on the internet.
They are internal loopbacks that point exclusively to your physical hardware,
a concept called localhost.
To share your project with the world, you can't just publish the laptop.
You must physically tear the frontend, backend, and database apart.
First, the frontend code flies to a global hosting service,
trading its internal port number for a real public domain name.
Next, the backend and database migrate to their own independent cloud service.
Because they no longer share the same physical computer,
you must rewrite their internal connection instructions so they can hunt each other down across the public internet.
Now anyone in the world can ping that public link,
safely routing their data through your newly connected cloud architecture.
Your invisible local app is finally a live global product.
