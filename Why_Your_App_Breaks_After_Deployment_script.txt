Your full-stack app works perfectly on your laptop.
Your React frontend talks to the ASP.NET backend seamlessly,
but the second you push it live, everything completely breaks.
Here are the three configuration traps that cause this exact failure.
First up, the localhost trap.
If you leave localhost URLs in your code,
the live frontend won't query your server.
It will query the user's own empty personal computer.
You must swap those connection strings for live cloud URLs during the deployment.
Next, the CORS block.
On your local machine, your front and backends communicate freely,
but live cloud servers automatically reject requests from different domains.
This security wall is called cross-origin resource sharing.
Unless you explicitly whitelist your new live URL, the backend will actively block your data.
Finally, the authentication collapse.
User logins rely on secret JWT keys.
If you forget to manually define those keys in your live server's production environment variables,
the API cannot validate any user sessions,
effectively locking everyone out of the app.
Deployment is never just about uploading code.
It's about completely rewiring your application's security and routing for the hostile public internet.
